# Diffusion-factor assessment: agentic AI for applicant support (evidence date 2026-10-25)

Sources are in `../evidence/source_register.json`. Grades: A = representative and recent; B = credible but partial; C = cannot set a stage.

## 1. Where is it on the curve? Each population separately

| Level | Population and measure | Best evidence | Placement (16% take-off test) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | U.S. adults; ever used / use generative AI | 34% ever used ChatGPT, Feb-Mar 2025 (D1, A); ~40% of 18-64s use genAI, late 2024 (D2, A); U.S. working-age 28.3% by telemetry, H2 2025 (D3, B) | **Early majority; past take-off.** Intensity low: 1-5% of work hours (D2) |
| L2a | U.S. firms; any AI in any business function, last 2 weeks | 19.8%, period ending May 2026; 17-20% since Dec 2025; 37% of 250+ employee firms (D4, A) | **At the take-off line**; early majority among large firms |
| L2b | Organizations; agentic AI in production or scaling | No representative measure. Global online executive survey (self-report): 23% scaling somewhere, ≤10% in any function (D5, B); 19% significant investment in a self-selected poll (D7, B) | **Early adopters; before the chasm** |
| L3a | Admissions/marketing/enrollment respondents; FAQ chatbots | 36% use chatbots for FAQs (D11, B); history since ~2016 (D19); peer professional-studies school since 2022 (D16); NYU Admissions genAI bot (D17) | **Past take-off** among surveyed admissions and marketing staff |
| L3b | Higher-ed institutions; tool-using recruitment agents (the proposed application) | Vendor pre-built skills, some beta (D15, C); one planned, vendor-reported deployment (D14, C); no share in any 2025-26 survey (D11-D13) | **Innovators / early adopters**; share not measurable |
| L4 | SPS peer group (professional/continuing education) and NYU | Small self-selected survey, no agent data (D13, C); NYU and SPS already run FAQ bots (D17, D18) | Peer stage unknown. **SPS: implementation stage for chatbots; knowledge/persuasion stage for agents** |

## 2. Rogers' five characteristics for L3b (baseline: FAQ chatbot plus staff)

| Characteristic | Rating | Observed or inferred | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative advantage | Unproven | Inferred | Added actions (requests, bookings, application starts). Only vendor projections exist (D14) |
| Compatibility | Mixed | Observed | Channel exists (D17, D18). Privacy/security is the top barrier (56%, D11); only 9% say policies cover AI (D12) |
| Complexity | Moderate-high | Inferred | Permissions, CRM integration, human approval; staff readiness a barrier for 50% of the peer sample (D13, C) |
| Trialability | High | Observed | Pre-built skills (D15); open standards MCP and A2A (D9, D10) |
| Observability | **Low** | Observed | No comparable institution has published measured agent outcomes; only announcements and projections (D14, D16) |

**The factor that matters most is observability.** A fast follower adopts on visible peer results, and none exist yet. Low observability also explains why diffusion is pushed by vendors and standards bodies (external influence) rather than pulled by peer imitation (internal influence).

## 3. Accelerators and inhibitors

| Accelerators | Inhibitors |
|---|---|
| Change agents: CRM/education vendors with pre-built agent skills (D15) | Accuracy and trust: 46% of developers distrust AI output (D8) |
| Standards: MCP with 10,000+ public servers (D9); A2A with 100+ supporting firms (D10) | Privacy and security, the top higher-ed barrier (D11, D12) |
| Complementary assets: existing chatbot channels and CRMs (D17, D18) | Unclear value; over 40% of agent projects forecast to be cancelled (D7) |
| L1 familiarity: most young applicants already use genAI (58% of under-30s, D1) | Hype outrunning use: agents at the Peak of Inflated Expectations (D6); "agent washing" (D7) |
| | Budget and infrastructure (76% and 64% in the peer sample, D13, C) |

## 4. Attention versus diffusion (Prep Lab 6)

| Attention signals (external) | Diffusion signals (adoption) |
|---|---|
| Hype-cycle peak (D6) | Census any-AI rate (D4) |
| Vendor launches and beta skills (D15) | Survey use rates (D1, D2, D11) |
| Standards announcements (D9, D10) | Peer chatbot deployments (D16, D17) |
| Forecasts (D7) | |
| Projections (D14) | |

For tool-using agents in admissions, the signals are almost entirely of the first kind. Vendor messaging is still "new" (beta labels, availability disclaimers), not "useful" with referenced outcomes (C1, slide 54).
