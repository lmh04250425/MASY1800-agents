# Team handoff: Emerging Technology Adoption Agent v1.1

1. **Agent package:** `agents/adoption_agent/`. It contains the instructions, four cases, eight archived outputs, the graded register and a verifier with 27 checks.

2. **Recommended adoption posture for NYU SPS:** **Proof of concept now**, gated to a **bounded experiment** within this admissions cycle. **Not adopt.**
   - The proof of concept is a sandbox comparison of staff-only, Violet or simple retrieval, and the agent, on about 300 approved or synthetic inquiries.
   - The gates are no unauthorized actions; errors no worse than the best baseline; a clear cost advantage; compliance sign-off; and confirmed approval capacity.

3. **Factor that most strongly drives it:** relative advantage over SPS's existing chatbot (Violet) is unproven, for an application the course scheme classes as **bleeding edge**. Rule 1 says do not adopt before the crossing; Rule 2 says pilot. As a fast follower facing low observability (A5), SPS has to create its own evidence.

4. **Condition most likely to reverse it:**
   - **Downward:** the proof of concept shows the agent does not beat Violet or simple retrieval on accuracy-adjusted cost per correctly completed inquiry. Then the posture becomes **reject for the present** (keep Violet).
   - **Upward:** comparable peers publish measured outcomes and vendors ship generally available recruitment agents with references. Then the class moves to leading edge and Rule 3 allows adoption.

5. **Issue passed to the Organizational Adoption agent (not resolved here):** can SPS admissions staff the human-approval and content-governance work without degrading service? That covers review-queue capacity, training, and who owns agent content and incidents. This decides whether the bounded experiment is operable at all.

**For the team proposal** ("Why should NYU SPS adopt Agentic AI recruiters now?"): the defensible "now" is to start the evidence program now, meaning the proof of concept and then a gated experiment, not production adoption. That is what the course's own adoption rules support for bleeding-edge technology.
